WHY THE EXPLANATION MATTERS

A calculator is only useful when you understand what it is measuring.

A monthly payment can look affordable while the total borrowing cost is high. A salary estimate can look precise while pension, tax-code or student-loan details change the payslip. A property tax estimate can be correct for one buyer situation and wrong for another.

That is why the main WorthChex pages explain the model, show the assumptions and link to the relevant source or methodology. The numbers are there to help you explore a scenario; the surrounding explanation helps you judge whether the scenario matches real life.

HOW TO USE WORTHCHEX

Three steps are usually more useful than one headline number.

1. Start with your real inputs

Use current income, actual regular spending, the quoted APR or rate where you have one, and a realistic time period. Avoid filling gaps with optimistic assumptions just to make the result look better.

2. Run a second scenario

Test a higher cost, a lower contribution, a shorter term or a larger emergency buffer. The difference between the two scenarios is often more informative than either headline result.

3. Read the limitations

Every model leaves something out. Before relying on the result, check the page's methodology section and the official source linked for rules that can change.
