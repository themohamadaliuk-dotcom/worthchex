HOW THE PERCENTAGES WORK

Transparent ratios instead of a made-up “affordability score”.

WorthChex does not turn your answers into a single score. The result shows the underlying maths so you can audit it: existing spending ÷ take-home income, new payment ÷ take-home income, and total monthly commitments ÷ take-home income. For a cash purchase, it also shows how much of the savings entered would be used and how much of the emergency target would remain covered.

Lenders use their own detailed affordability assessments based on income, expenditure and other circumstances. FCA material states that affordability assessments consider income and expenditure rather than relying on one universal income or expenditure threshold. Read FCA guidance on mortgage affordability →

HOW TO READ THE RESULT

Affordability is a budget calculation, not a single percentage.

Existing spending %

This is the share of your take-home income already used by the costs you entered. A complete figure is more useful than a low figure built from missing costs.

New payment %

For finance, this shows the new monthly payment as a percentage of take-home income. It is displayed separately so you can see exactly what the purchase adds.

Total commitments %

This combines your entered spending with the new payment. It is the clearest percentage for seeing how much of your monthly income has already been allocated in the scenario.

Why the calculator does not use a universal pass/fail percentage:

MoneyHelper says people typically spend around 28–35% of income on a mortgage, but it also says there is no specific percentage that is right for everyone. A car payment, personal loan or cash purchase should not automatically be judged using a mortgage rule of thumb. Your actual income, spending and other commitments matter.

WHAT TO INCLUDE

The biggest accuracy improvement is complete inputs.

Include annual insurance, servicing, school costs, subscriptions and other occasional spending by converting them into a monthly amount. If you spend £600 during the year on a cost that is not paid monthly, budgeting roughly £50 a month keeps the calculation from overstating the spare cash in an ordinary month.

For a vehicle purchase, remember that the finance payment is not the same as the cost of owning the vehicle. Insurance, fuel, tyres, servicing, parking, tax and repairs can sit outside the finance agreement.

For borrowing, compare the quoted APR and actual agreement with the calculator. The calculator can model the mathematics; it cannot see lender-specific underwriting, fees, promotional offers or contract terms.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Affordability calculator FAQs

Does this tell me what a lender will approve?

No. Lenders use their own affordability tests, income verification, credit information, product rules and other criteria. WorthChex shows the household-budget maths from the figures you enter.

What percentages should I look at first?

Start with existing spending %, then the new payment % if you are financing, and finally total commitments % and remaining income %. These figures show where your money is going without hiding the calculation behind a score.

Should I use gross salary or take-home pay?

Use take-home pay for this calculator because the spending decision is based on the money available to your household after payroll deductions.

Why do you ask for annual or irregular costs?

Because a monthly budget can look artificially generous when annual insurance, repairs, servicing, holidays or similar costs are missing. Converting those costs into a monthly allowance makes the scenario more realistic.

What if I do not know my emergency-fund target?

Leave it blank or enter the amount you want to keep available. The result will show the savings position without pretending there is a universal emergency-fund number.
