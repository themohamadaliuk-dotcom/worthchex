METHODOLOGY

Cash and finance are assessed differently.

A cash purchase is checked against your entered savings and emergency target. A financed purchase is checked against recurring monthly disposable income and the modelled finance payment.

This is a planning indicator, not a lender affordability decision.

HOW TO READ THE RESULT

Affordability is more than a monthly payment.

A purchase can fit a monthly budget and still leave too little cash in reserve. It can also leave a healthy savings balance while creating a recurring payment that makes the rest of the month uncomfortable. This calculator keeps those two questions separate.

Monthly breathing room

This is the money left after the spending categories you enter. It is not a guarantee that you will actually have that amount available every month because irregular costs may not be included.

Savings safety

For a cash purchase, compare the post-purchase balance with the emergency-fund target you entered. A mathematically affordable purchase may still use savings you intended to keep available.

Finance cost

For finance, look at both the monthly payment and the total borrowing cost. A longer term may lower the monthly payment while keeping the debt around for longer.

Example: a £20,000 purchase might be manageable with a large monthly surplus but uncomfortable as a cash purchase if it uses most of the savings buffer. The same purchase financed over a longer period may preserve cash while creating a new monthly commitment. Running both scenarios makes that trade-off visible.
WHAT TO INCLUDE

Use realistic inputs, not optimistic ones.

For a useful result, include regular costs that are easy to forget when building a budget. Housing, utilities, groceries, transport, debt payments and subscriptions are obvious examples. Irregular costs such as annual insurance, servicing, repairs, gifts, holidays or professional expenses may need a separate monthly allowance in your own budget.

For vehicle purchases, also consider insurance, fuel, maintenance, tyres, parking and road-related costs. The calculator can help you test the purchase price and finance payment; it cannot automatically know your future running costs.

When to run the calculation again

Run another scenario when your deposit changes, the APR changes, your income changes, a regular cost is missing, or you want to protect a larger emergency buffer. Comparing two realistic cases is usually more informative than trying to find one “maximum” price.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Affordability calculator FAQs

Does this tell me what a lender will approve?

No. Lenders use their own affordability tests, income verification, credit information, product rules and other criteria. WorthChex is a household planning tool.

Should I use gross salary or take-home pay?

Use the money you actually have available for your monthly budget. For most household affordability decisions, take-home pay is the more useful starting point.

What if I do not know my emergency-fund target?

Use a cautious figure that reflects the amount you want to keep available after the purchase. You can then run a second scenario with a larger or smaller buffer to see how sensitive the result is.

Should I include existing debt payments?

Yes. If a debt payment is a recurring commitment, including it gives the calculator a more realistic view of remaining monthly room.
