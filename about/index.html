ABOUT WORTHCHEX

Tools that explain the number, not just calculate it.

WorthChex is a UK-focused financial calculator and information site. The aim is straightforward: give visitors a useful calculation, explain what sits behind it, and make the assumptions visible so the result can be understood rather than taken as a magic answer.

What we build

WorthChex covers everyday decisions such as budgeting, saving, borrowing, salary and take-home pay, mortgages, car finance, VAT and residential Stamp Duty Land Tax. The calculators are designed to work in the browser without requiring a bank connection or an account.

How we write

Important concepts are explained in plain language, with worked examples and limitations where they help a visitor interpret the result. We avoid presenting a planning estimate as a lender decision, tax assessment or personalised financial advice.

How we use sources

For changeable UK rules, the site links to primary sources such as GOV.UK and other established public guidance. The methodology page explains the main calculation models and the boundaries of the tools.

Our content principles

Useful before monetised

The information on a page should make sense even when the calculator or advertising is ignored. A visitor should be able to learn something from the page itself.

Transparent assumptions

When a model simplifies a real-world decision, the page should say what has and has not been included. This is especially important for tax, borrowing, property and investment-related calculations.

Practical internal links

Guides link to relevant calculators and calculators link back to explanations. The goal is to help visitors move from a question to the right tool without getting lost.

EDITORIAL & REVIEW PROCESS

How WorthChex keeps the information useful.

Question-led pages

Guides are built around a real visitor question and aim to answer it directly before asking the visitor to use a calculator. A page should contain explanation, examples, trade-offs and limitations rather than a collection of search terms.

Source-led updates

Rules that can change, including tax, National Insurance, Stamp Duty and VAT, are checked against the relevant official guidance when affected pages are reviewed. The page links to the source so the reader can verify the rule for themselves.

Calculator checks

Calculation changes are supported by the site's regression tests and methodology documentation. The aim is to keep the maths consistent with the explanation instead of treating the calculator as a black box.

Where a material rule changes, the relevant page is updated rather than silently relying on an old example. Guide pages may display a review date so readers can see when the editorial information was last checked.

What WorthChex is not

WorthChex does not provide a personalised financial plan, approve credit, issue mortgages, decide tax liabilities, or guarantee investment returns. Results are estimates based on the information and assumptions shown on the relevant page.

For consequential decisions, check the current official guidance or the actual offer, statement or contract you are relying on. Where a calculation depends on facts that a calculator cannot know, treat the result as a planning scenario rather than a definitive answer.
