UNDERSTANDING LOAN COST

A lower monthly payment can cost more overall.

For a standard amortising loan, the monthly payment contains both interest and principal. Extending the term spreads the borrowing across more months, which can reduce the payment but increase the total interest charged over the life of the loan.

APR

Use the representative APR from the actual offer where possible. It is more useful for comparing borrowing costs than looking at a nominal rate in isolation.

Fees

A fee added to the borrowing increases the amount being financed in this model. Read the lender's actual fee terms because products can treat fees differently.

Overpayments

Testing an extra monthly amount shows the potential effect on interest and payoff time. Actual early-repayment rules depend on the credit agreement.

Example: a £10,000 loan at 8.9% over five years is not just a £10,000 decision. The rate and term determine how much interest accumulates, while a fee or extra payment can move the total in either direction.
BORROWING CHECKLIST

Before taking a loan, look beyond the payment.

Compare the total amount payable, APR, fees, term and any early-repayment conditions you are offered. Then check the monthly payment against your existing household budget rather than assuming the lender's maximum is affordable.

Also check whether the purpose of the borrowing can be met in a cheaper way, whether you need the full amount and whether a shorter term remains manageable. The calculator is there to show the mathematics of the scenario, not to tell you whether borrowing is suitable.

Use a second scenario

Run the quoted offer first. Then try a £50 or £100 higher monthly payment, or a shorter term. Seeing how much time and interest changes can help you understand the value of flexibility in the repayment plan.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Personal loan calculator FAQs

What does APR tell me?

APR is designed to express the annual cost of borrowing and can include certain fees. Use the lender's representative figure when comparing actual offers.

Does a longer term always make a loan cheaper?

No. It may reduce the monthly payment but can increase the total interest because the balance is outstanding for longer.

Can I always overpay a personal loan?

Early-repayment rights and charges depend on the agreement and applicable rules. Check the actual lender terms before relying on an overpayment plan.

Should I compare monthly payments or total cost?

Both. The monthly payment tells you about affordability; total cost tells you what the borrowing may cost over the full modelled term.
