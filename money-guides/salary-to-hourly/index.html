SALARY GUIDE

How do you convert an annual salary into an hourly rate?

A simple annual-to-hourly conversion divides annual pay by the number of paid working hours in a year. The result is only as useful as the working-hours assumption underneath it.

THE SIMPLE FORMULA

Gross hourly rate = gross annual salary ÷ annual paid hours.

For a quick 40-hour-week example, 52 weeks gives 2,080 hours. £40,000 ÷ 2,080 is about £19.23 gross per hour.

Annual salary

Use the gross salary from the employment offer when comparing headline pay.

Working hours

Use contracted or realistically paid hours. A 35-hour week produces a different hourly figure from a 40-hour week.

Net hourly value

Take-home pay after selected deductions gives a different hourly figure from gross pay and is more useful for spending comparisons.

HOURS MATTER

Two jobs with the same salary can have different hourly values.

A job paying £40,000 for 35 hours a week has fewer contracted hours than one paying £40,000 for 40 hours. Annual leave, unpaid breaks, overtime expectations and unpaid weeks can also change the real comparison.

Example: if a £40,000 salary is divided by 2,080 hours, the simple gross rate is about £19.23 per hour. If the working year is based on 1,820 hours instead, the hourly figure is about £21.98. The salary did not change; the hours did.

When comparing job offers, consider the whole package, including pension, paid leave, commute and benefits, rather than using hourly pay as the only measure.

GROSS VS TAKE-HOME

Your bank-account hourly value is a separate calculation.

Income Tax, National Insurance, pension contributions and student-loan deductions can reduce take-home pay. WorthChex's Salary Calculator shows hourly equivalents after the selected deductions, so you can keep the gross and net comparisons separate.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Salary-to-hourly FAQs

Do I divide salary by 12 to get monthly pay?

Dividing an annual salary by 12 gives a gross monthly average. A real payslip can vary by pay frequency and deductions.

Should I subtract holidays when calculating hourly pay?

It depends on what you are comparing. For a simple contracted-pay equivalent, use paid working hours. For an effective-work-time comparison, unpaid time and additional hours can matter.

Is £40,000 always £19.23 an hour?

No. £19.23 comes from the specific assumption of £40,000 divided by 2,080 hours. Change the hours and the result changes.
