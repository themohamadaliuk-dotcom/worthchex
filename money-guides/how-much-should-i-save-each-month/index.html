SAVINGS GUIDE

How much should I save each month?

There is no universal percentage that works for every household. The useful contribution is one that moves you toward an important goal without repeatedly forcing you to dip back into savings.

START WITH A TARGET

The goal and deadline tell you what pace you need.

Choose a target, subtract the money you already have, and divide the remaining gap by the number of months available. That gives a simple contribution before interest or growth.

Example: if you have £2,000 and want £10,000 in two years, £8,000 remains. Dividing £8,000 by 24 months gives about £333.33 per month before interest.

The Savings Calculator can solve the same problem with an optional growth assumption or work backwards from a target date.

CHECK THE HOUSEHOLD BUDGET

A savings target should fit after essential costs.

Essential spending

Start with housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance, debt payments and other commitments that are difficult to reduce quickly.

Irregular costs

Leave room for annual or occasional expenses. A plan that ignores them may look affordable until the next insurance bill or repair arrives.

Flexibility

Consider what happens if income changes or spending rises. A slightly slower contribution can be more sustainable if it avoids withdrawals.

For a household-level check, the Affordability Calculator can show how much monthly room remains after the spending categories you enter.

INCREASE GRADUALLY

Consistency often matters more than the first month's ambition.

Start with an amount you can maintain, then review it after a pay rise, a completed debt payment or the end of another regular expense. Automating the contribution can also make a realistic plan easier to maintain.

Do not assume that investment returns or savings interest will make an aggressive plan work. Use the growth assumption as a scenario and compare it with a 0% baseline.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Monthly savings FAQs

Is saving 20% of income a rule?

Percentages can be useful as a budgeting reference, but they are not a universal requirement. Housing costs, debt, dependants and goals can make the same percentage very different in practice.

Should I save before paying debt?

That depends on the debt cost and your need for accessible cash. A small emergency buffer can help avoid new borrowing while you address expensive debt.

Should I count interest in my target?

You can model it, but compare with a 0% scenario so you can see how much the plan depends on a rate that could change.
