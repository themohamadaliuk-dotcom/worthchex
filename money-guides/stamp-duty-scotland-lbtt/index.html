Stamp Duty in Scotland: how LBTT works

Scotland does not use Stamp Duty. Home buyers pay Land and Buildings Transaction Tax (LBTT) instead, with different bands, its own first-time buyer relief and an 8% supplement on additional homes.

Tax year: 2026/27Last reviewed: October 2026
CURRENT RATES

LBTT residential rates.

Like Stamp Duty, LBTT is charged in slices: each rate applies only to the part of the price inside that band.

Price bandRate
£0 to £145,0000%
£145,000 to £250,0002%
£250,000 to £325,0005%
£325,000 to £750,00010%
Above £750,00012%

First-time buyers get a larger 0% band, up to £175,000. That saves up to £600 and there is no price cap.

Additional homes pay the Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS): 8% of the whole price, on top of normal LBTT, for purchases of £40,000 or more.

GOOD TO KNOW

LBTT rules that catch people out.

  • Replacing your main home: if you buy a new main home before selling your old one, you usually pay ADS up front and can reclaim it if you sell the previous home within 36 months.
  • Joint buyers: first-time buyer relief needs every buyer to be a first-time buyer.
  • Deadline: the return and payment are due within 30 days of the effective date, usually completion. Your solicitor normally handles this.
  • Compared with England: on cheaper homes LBTT can be lower than SDLT, but above about £325,000 it rises faster because of the 10% band.
FREQUENT QUESTIONS

LBTT FAQs

Is LBTT the same as Stamp Duty?

It does the same job, but it is a separate Scottish tax with different bands, administered by Revenue Scotland rather than HMRC.

Do first-time buyers pay LBTT?

Only on the part of the price above £175,000. A first-time buyer paying £175,000 or less pays no LBTT.

What is the Additional Dwelling Supplement?

An extra 8% of the whole purchase price when you buy an additional home for £40,000 or more, such as a second home or buy-to-let.
