DEBT GUIDE

How long does it take to pay off £5,000 of debt?

The answer depends heavily on the interest rate and the payment you can sustain each month. The same £5,000 balance can behave very differently at different APRs.

START WITH THE ZERO-INTEREST BASELINE

At 0%, £5,000 divided by £200 is 25 months.

That simple calculation gives a useful baseline. Real borrowing normally includes interest, so the actual timeline can be longer and the total amount repaid can be higher.

At 0% interest, £5,000 would require 50 monthly payments.

£200 a month

At 0% interest, the simple timeline is 25 monthly payments.

£300 a month

At 0% interest, the simple timeline is 17 monthly payments after rounding up.

THEN ADD THE APR

Interest changes both the date and the amount you pay.

Under a simple fixed-rate model, monthly interest is applied to the remaining balance and the payment then reduces that balance. A higher APR means a larger interest charge while the balance is high.

Important check: if the payment you can afford is lower than the interest being added, the balance will not fall under the model. That is why an apparently manageable monthly payment can fail to clear the debt.

Use your actual APR and payment if you know them, then run a second scenario with an extra payment. The Debt Payoff Calculator is designed for that comparison.

MAKE THE PLAN SUSTAINABLE

Do not choose a payment that breaks the rest of your budget.

A faster payoff can reduce interest, but essential spending still has to be covered. Test the debt payment alongside your household budget and leave room for unexpected costs so the plan is less likely to be abandoned.

If there are several debts with different rates, consider modelling them separately because one average APR can hide the most expensive balance.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

£5,000 debt payoff FAQs

How quickly can I clear £5,000?

It depends on the APR and monthly payment. At 0% the arithmetic is simply the balance divided by the payment, but interest makes real timelines longer.

Does paying £50 extra matter?

It can. Reducing principal sooner can reduce the number of months on which interest is charged. Use the calculator to quantify the effect under your assumed APR.

What if my debt has a variable rate?

A fixed-rate calculation is only a scenario. Run several rates if you want to see how sensitive the payoff plan is to future changes.
