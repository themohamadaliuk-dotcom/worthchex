AFFORDABILITY GUIDE

How much car can I afford?

The useful question is not simply whether a lender will approve the finance. It is whether the car fits your household budget while leaving room for normal life, savings and unexpected costs.

START WITH THE HOUSEHOLD

Calculate the money left after your regular commitments.

Begin with take-home income, then subtract housing, utilities, food, transport, debt payments, subscriptions and other regular costs. The money left is a planning measure of disposable income, not a promise that every month will be identical.

Purchase price

The sticker price is the starting point, not the complete cost. A deposit changes the cash impact and amount financed.

Finance payment

For borrowing, test the monthly payment and total finance cost. The term and APR can make two cars with the same price behave very differently.

Running costs

Insurance, fuel, servicing, tyres, parking, repairs and breakdown cover can all matter after the finance payment is gone.

CASH VS FINANCE

A car can be affordable monthly but uncomfortable as a cash purchase.

For a cash purchase, the main question is how much savings remain afterwards. For finance, the main question is whether the recurring payment still leaves enough monthly room. Those are different forms of affordability.

Example: suppose a £20,000 car is affordable under a finance plan because the payment fits after regular expenses. Paying £20,000 from savings could still be a problem if it would remove most of the emergency buffer. Running both cases can reveal the trade-off.

A PRACTICAL CAR BUDGET

Do not budget from a lender's maximum.

A lender can assess whether a particular finance agreement passes its own affordability criteria, but that does not tell you what amount leaves your household comfortable. Build your own number from the spending you expect to continue after buying the car.

Run a second scenario

Try a lower purchase price, a larger deposit, a shorter term or a more conservative monthly budget. If the car only works under the most optimistic scenario, that tells you something useful about the risk in the plan.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

How much car can I afford? FAQs

Should I use gross or take-home salary?

For household affordability, take-home income is usually more useful because it reflects the money available after payroll deductions.

Should I include car running costs?

Yes. The finance payment is only one part of vehicle ownership. Include the running costs that are relevant to the car and your circumstances.

Is a bigger deposit always better?

Not necessarily. A bigger deposit can reduce borrowing, but using too much cash may leave too small an emergency buffer.

Can the calculator tell me the exact car I should buy?

No. It can compare scenarios. The final decision depends on your needs, running costs, finance terms and wider financial priorities.
