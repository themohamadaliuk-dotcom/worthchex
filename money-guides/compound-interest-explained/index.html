SAVINGS & GROWTH GUIDE

What is compound interest?

Compound growth means that returns can themselves contribute to future growth. Over long periods, the effect can become much larger than simple growth on the original amount alone.

THE FOUR MAIN INPUTS

Starting balance, contributions, rate and time all matter.

Starting balance

The amount already saved gives the calculation a starting base. A larger initial balance means more money is exposed to the assumed growth from the first period.

Regular contributions

Adding money regularly can be a major part of the final value. Contributions are not “interest”; they are new money being added to the plan.

Rate and time

The assumed annual rate and the number of years determine how often the balance can grow. Small changes in the assumption can create large differences over long periods.

Contribution frequency and compounding frequency can also change the illustration because money enters and earns growth at different points in time.

A SIMPLE EXAMPLE

Why long horizons can change the mix.

Imagine starting with £2,000 and contributing £250 each month. After one year, most of the balance will have come from your starting amount and new contributions. Over a much longer period, the accumulated growth has more time to compound on earlier growth as well as on the contributions.

Important distinction: if the assumed rate is 0%, the result is simply the starting balance plus contributions. Any value above that in a positive-rate scenario is the modelled growth component.

SAVINGS VS INVESTING

A compound-growth illustration is not a forecast.

A fixed rate is convenient for explaining the maths, but actual savings rates can change. Investments introduce another level of uncertainty because returns can rise and fall and are not guaranteed.

For a planning calculation, compare multiple scenarios: a 0% or low-growth case, a middle assumption and a more optimistic case. The range can be more useful than treating one rate as certain.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Compound interest FAQs

Does compound interest mean my savings double every few years?

No. The growth rate determines the timeline, and adding contributions changes the result further. A fixed-rate calculator is a mathematical illustration, not a universal rule.

Is compound interest guaranteed?

No. A savings account rate can change and investment returns are not guaranteed.

Does tax affect compound growth?

It can, depending on the product and your circumstances. This WorthChex model focuses on the growth arithmetic and does not calculate personal tax on savings or investments.

Why does time matter so much?

Growth is applied repeatedly. Earlier growth can then form part of the balance used for later growth, which is the core of compounding.
