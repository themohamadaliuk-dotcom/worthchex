SAVINGS GUIDE

Compound interest explained, with real numbers

Compound interest is interest earned on your interest. Each year's growth is added to the balance, so next year you earn on a bigger amount. £10,000 at 4% becomes about £22,226 after 20 years without adding another penny.

Last reviewed: October 2026Figures: worked out with the WorthChex calculators
SIMPLE VS COMPOUND

£10,000 at 5% for 20 years.

Simple interest£20,0005% of £10,000 each year
Compounded yearly£26,533+£6,533 more
Compounded monthly£27,126+£7,126 more

With simple interest you only ever earn on the original £10,000, so you get £500 a year. With compound interest, year two earns 5% on £10,500, year three on £11,025, and so on. The gap grows every year.

Over 30 years at 7%, most of the final pot is growth, not money you paid in. That is why time matters more than almost anything else. (7% is an illustration of long-term investing; investments can fall as well as rise and are not guaranteed.)

Starting early: Alex saves £200 a month from age 25 to 35, then stops. Sam saves £200 a month from 35 to 65. At 6% a year, at 65 Alex has about £197,395 from £24,000 paid in. Sam has about £200,903 from £72,000 paid in. Alex's 10 early years do almost as much work as Sam's 30.
RULE OF 72

A quick way to estimate doubling time.

Divide 72 by the yearly rate to estimate how many years it takes money to double.

RateRule of 72 estimateActual (yearly compounding)
3%24.0 years23.4 years
4%18.0 years17.7 years
6%12.0 years11.9 years
8%9.0 years9.0 years

Inflation works the same way in reverse. At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years, so savings earning less than inflation lose buying power over time.

MAKE IT WORK FOR YOU

Practical ways to use compounding.

  • Keep interest in the account rather than paying it out, so it compounds.
  • Use your ISA allowance: you can put up to £20,000 a year into ISAs in 2026/27 and the growth is tax-free.
  • Watch the Personal Savings Allowance: outside an ISA, basic-rate taxpayers can earn £1,000 of interest a year tax-free, higher-rate £500 and additional-rate £0.
  • Compounding works against you on debt: unpaid credit card interest is added to the balance and then charged interest too. See how debt interest works.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Compound interest FAQs

How often is interest compounded?

Savings accounts usually calculate interest daily and pay it monthly or yearly. The AER (Annual Equivalent Rate) already includes the effect of compounding, so you can compare accounts directly.

Is compound interest guaranteed?

On a fixed-rate savings account the rate is fixed for the term. Variable rates can change, and investment returns are not guaranteed at all.

What is the difference between AER and APR?

AER shows what you earn on savings, including compounding. APR shows what you pay on borrowing, including fees.
