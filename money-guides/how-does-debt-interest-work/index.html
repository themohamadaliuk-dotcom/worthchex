DEBT GUIDE

How does debt interest work?

Interest is one of the main reasons a debt balance can take longer to clear than the simple balance divided by the payment. Understanding the sequence makes payoff calculations easier to interpret.

A SIMPLE MONTHLY MODEL

Interest is calculated from the balance before the payment reduces it.

For a simplified fixed-rate model, monthly interest can be represented as the remaining balance multiplied by the annual rate divided by 12. The payment then covers that interest, with the rest reducing the principal.

Illustration: a £5,000 balance at 20% annual rate produces a simple first-month interest estimate of about £83.33 before the payment is applied. A £200 payment would therefore reduce principal by roughly £116.67 in that first-month model.

Real credit products can calculate interest differently, especially when balances, payment dates and rates change. The example is for understanding the mechanics behind the WorthChex planning model.

WHY PAYMENTS MATTER

The early months can have a larger interest component.

When the balance is high, a fixed annual rate applied to that balance produces a larger interest charge. As principal falls, the interest portion normally falls too under a simple fixed-rate amortisation model.

Higher payment

More of each month's payment can go toward principal, shortening the payoff timeline and usually reducing total interest.

Longer timeline

A smaller payment may keep the balance outstanding for more months, creating more opportunities for interest to accumulate.

Rate matters

Two debts with the same balance can have very different payoff costs if the annual rates differ.

APR IS NOT THE WHOLE CONTRACT

Use the lender's actual terms for the real debt.

A calculator can illustrate interest using one APR, but real accounts may have promotional periods, variable rates, minimum payments, fees or other contractual details. Where the actual agreement differs from the simple model, the agreement should take priority.

For multiple debts, model them separately when the rates or payment rules differ. Combining different balances under one average APR can hide the most expensive part of the debt.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Debt-interest FAQs

Why does dividing the balance by the payment give the wrong answer?

Because interest is added during the payoff period. The monthly payment has to cover the interest charge as well as reduce principal.

Does paying extra earlier help?

Under a fixed-rate amortisation model, reducing principal earlier reduces the balance on which later interest is calculated. Actual lender rules determine how overpayments are applied.

What if my payment is lower than the interest?

The balance may not fall under the model. The WorthChex calculator flags this rather than showing a misleading payoff date.

Does APR tell me the exact interest my lender charges each month?

Not necessarily. APR is a broader annual measure of borrowing cost. The contractual calculation method and fees determine the actual account behaviour.
