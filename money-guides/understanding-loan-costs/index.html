BORROWING GUIDE

How to understand the real cost of a loan.

A monthly repayment tells you what leaves your bank account each month. It does not, by itself, tell you what the borrowing costs in total. APR, interest, fees, term and repayment rules all affect the answer.

Reviewed: 19 September 2026 · General educational information. Check the actual credit agreement before relying on a borrowing calculation.

THE FIVE NUMBERS

Look at the whole borrowing package.

Amount borrowed

Borrowing more increases the amount on which interest is charged. Ask whether you need the full amount or whether part of the purchase can be delayed or funded another way.

APR

APR is intended to express the annual cost of borrowing and can incorporate certain fees. Use the representative APR in an actual offer when comparing products.

Term

A longer term can reduce the monthly payment while keeping the balance outstanding for longer. That can increase total interest.

Fees

Arrangement or other fees can affect the total cost. Check whether a fee is paid upfront, added to the balance or treated differently by the lender.

Total amount payable

This is often the clearest number for understanding the overall cost of the borrowing under the stated assumptions.

THE MATHS

Why a longer term can cost more.

For a standard amortising loan, the balance is reduced gradually. Each payment contains an interest component and a principal component. The interest is calculated from the outstanding balance, so keeping that balance for more months generally means paying interest for more months.

Illustration: suppose two scenarios borrow the same £10,000 at the same rate. A five-year term spreads repayment across 60 monthly payments; a three-year term spreads it across 36. The shorter term usually requires a higher monthly payment, but the balance is reduced sooner, so the total interest can be lower.

This trade-off is more informative than asking only whether a monthly payment “looks affordable”. The WorthChex Personal Loan Calculator lets you change the term and compare the modelled cost.

APR IS NOT THE WHOLE STORY

Compare like with like.

APR helps compare the annual cost of credit, but an individual decision can still depend on the loan amount, term, fees, repayment structure and early-repayment conditions. A lower monthly payment is not automatically cheaper, and a lower headline interest rate is not meaningful if the products being compared have different fees or terms.

Same amount, different term

Hold the amount and rate constant, then change the term. This isolates the effect of repayment time.

Same term, different APR

Hold the amount and term constant, then change the rate. This shows how sensitive the payment and total interest are to the borrowing rate.

Fee added to borrowing

When a fee is financed rather than paid separately, you can end up paying interest on the fee as well. The actual agreement determines the treatment.

BEFORE YOU BORROW

First test whether the repayment survives a difficult month.

MoneyHelper recommends considering whether you could continue making repayments if costs rise or income falls, and comparing the rate, APR, total repayment and fees. Read the MoneyHelper borrowing guide →

A useful personal check is to put the proposed payment into your household budget, then repeat the budget with higher essential costs or lower income. The aim is not to predict the future; it is to see whether the plan has any margin.

Do not ignore existing commitments

A new loan sits alongside existing rent or mortgage payments, utilities, insurance, transport and other debt. If those commitments are already close to the household's reliable income, a calculator can show the mathematics but cannot make the borrowing affordable.

The result does not predict approval, credit scoring, underwriting or the exact terms a lender will offer.

OVERPAYMENTS

An extra £50 can be more informative than a new headline rate.

When an agreement permits overpayments, testing a small extra monthly amount can show how much time and interest may change. This is particularly useful when choosing between a comfortable standard payment and a payment you could make consistently.

Actual early-repayment charges, limits and rules vary by agreement. Never assume that a calculator's overpayment scenario is permitted by a particular lender.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Understanding loan costs FAQs

Should I focus on the monthly payment?

Use the monthly payment for budgeting, but also compare APR, fees, total amount payable and term. A low payment can be produced by spreading the borrowing over more months.

Can a fee make a loan more expensive?

Yes. A fee adds to the cost of borrowing and may itself be financed depending on the agreement.

Does a lower interest rate always mean a cheaper loan?

Not necessarily when products have different fees, terms or borrowing amounts. Compare the complete offer on a like-for-like basis.

Can I use this guide to decide whether I should borrow?

The guide explains the cost structure. Whether borrowing is suitable depends on your circumstances, budget and the actual agreement.
