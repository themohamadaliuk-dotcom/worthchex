SALARY GUIDE

What is salary sacrifice?

Salary sacrifice is an arrangement where an employee gives up part of their gross salary in return for an employer-provided benefit. Depending on the benefit and the arrangement, the sacrificed amount can change the pay used for some tax and National Insurance calculations.

THE BASIC IDEA

Gross pay can change before the payroll deductions are calculated.

In a salary-sacrifice arrangement, the employee agrees to a lower contractual cash salary in exchange for a benefit. A common example is a salary-sacrifice pension, although the exact treatment varies by benefit and scheme.

Cash salary

This is the amount of salary you receive as pay rather than as the sacrificed benefit.

Benefit

The employer provides or arranges the benefit in exchange for the sacrificed amount.

Payroll effect

The lower cash salary can change the amount used for certain payroll calculations, depending on the benefit and current rules.

WHY TAKE-HOME PAY CAN CHANGE

The net reduction can be smaller than the gross sacrifice.

Suppose an employee gives up part of gross salary for a qualifying arrangement. If the sacrificed amount no longer counts in the same way for Income Tax and, where applicable, employee National Insurance, the employee can pay less in those deductions. That means take-home pay can fall by less than the gross amount sacrificed.

Important: a £100 gross salary sacrifice does not mean take-home pay necessarily falls by exactly £100. The actual difference depends on the employee's tax position, National Insurance, pension method and the benefit involved.

CHECK THE SCHEME

Not every benefit has the same tax treatment.

Salary sacrifice rules depend on the benefit and the arrangement. Before joining a scheme, check the employer documentation, what happens to contractual salary, pension or other benefits, and how the arrangement affects your actual payslip.

Current tax guidance is published by GOV.UK. Check that salary changes do not affect applicable minimum-wage requirements and use your employer's scheme rules for the specific benefit.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Salary-sacrifice FAQs

Does salary sacrifice always increase take-home pay?

No. The result depends on what you sacrifice, the tax treatment of the benefit and your payroll circumstances.

Is salary sacrifice the same as a net-pay pension?

No. They are different pension/payroll arrangements. The way the contribution is deducted affects the calculation.

Can salary sacrifice affect other benefits?

It can affect salary-linked calculations or employer benefits depending on the scheme. Check how your employer defines pensionable or contractual salary.

Can I calculate the exact amount online?

You can model a planning scenario, but your employer's payroll and scheme documentation should be treated as the authoritative source for the real deduction.
