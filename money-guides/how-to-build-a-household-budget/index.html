BUDGETING GUIDE

How to build a household budget

A household budget brings everyone's income and every shared cost into one plan. Below are six steps, then a full worked example for a couple with one child earning £38,000 and £26,000, who take home about £4,213 a month after a 5% pension.

Last reviewed: October 2026Figures: worked out with the WorthChex calculators
SIX STEPS

Build it in an evening.

  1. Add up take-home income for everyone who contributes: salaries after tax, pension and student loan, plus Child Benefit and any other regular income.
  2. List fixed costs from three months of bank statements: mortgage or rent, Council Tax, bills, insurance, childcare, debt payments.
  3. Estimate variable costs like food and fuel from the same three months. Use the average, not your best month.
  4. Add an annual costs pot: total up yearly and irregular costs and divide by 12.
  5. Decide savings first, then fit spending around what is left.
  6. Agree how you split it. Couples often use a joint account for shared bills, funded in proportion to income.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Household budget FAQs

What if our budget does not balance?

List costs from biggest to smallest and look at the top five first: housing, childcare, car, food and energy. Switching deals at renewal and claiming entitlements usually makes more difference than cutting small treats.

Should we include pension contributions?

Use take-home pay, which already has workplace pension contributions taken off, so you do not count them twice.

How often should we review it?

Monthly at first, then at least every six months and whenever a deal, salary or family situation changes.
