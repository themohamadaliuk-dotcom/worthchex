HOW IT WORKS

How take-home pay is worked out.

  1. Take off the tax-free Personal Allowance (£12,570 for most people).
  2. Tax the rest in slices: 20% on the first £37,700 of taxable income, then 40%, then 45%. Only the slice in each band is taxed at that rate.
  3. Work out National Insurance separately: 8% between £12,570 and £50,270, then 2% above.
  4. Take off any pension contribution and student loan repayment.
  5. What is left is your take-home pay.
Example, £40,000: £27,430 is taxable, so Income Tax is £5,486. National Insurance is 8% of £27,430 = £2,194. Take-home pay is £32,320 a year, or £2,693 a month.
FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Salary after tax FAQs

Why is my take-home pay lower than this table?

The table has no pension or student loan. A 5% workplace pension and a student loan can each reduce monthly pay by £100 or more. Your tax code, benefits in kind or other payroll deductions can also make a difference.

Does a pay rise ever reduce take-home pay?

No. Tax is charged in bands, so moving into a higher band only affects the money above the threshold. Every pay rise increases take-home pay, although between £100,000 and £125,140 you keep only about 38p of each extra pound.

Is take-home pay different in Scotland?

Yes. Scotland has six Income Tax bands. People earning under about £30,000 pay slightly less than elsewhere in the UK, and people earning more pay more. National Insurance is the same across the UK.

How do I work out my hourly rate?

Divide your annual salary by the number of hours you work in a year. For a 37.5-hour week that is 1,950 hours, so £30,000 is about £15.38 an hour before tax.
