MORTGAGE GUIDE

How much mortgage can I afford?

A lender's maximum is not the same as a comfortable household budget. A useful affordability estimate starts with the money actually available after regular costs and then tests the mortgage under different assumptions.

START WITH THE HOUSEHOLD BUDGET

Take-home income is only the beginning.

List the income you can reasonably rely on and subtract housing-related costs, utilities, food, transport, existing debt, subscriptions and other recurring commitments. Then make room for irregular costs that do not arrive every month but still have to be paid during the year.

Existing commitments

Existing loans, credit cards and other fixed payments reduce the money available for a new mortgage.

Deposit and LTV

A larger deposit reduces the mortgage needed and the starting loan-to-value. It can also change which mortgage products are available.

Rate and term

The interest rate affects the monthly cost while the term spreads repayment across time. A longer term can lower the payment but increase total interest.

MODEL TWO DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

Use affordability and mortgage calculations together.

The Affordability Calculator is about the household: how much monthly room remains after the spending you enter. The Mortgage Calculator is about the borrowing: how property price, deposit, rate, term and overpayments change the mortgage payment and interest.

Example: two households might qualify for a similar mortgage amount but have very different monthly budgets because one has childcare, car finance or variable income. A household-level calculation can therefore tell a different story from a lender maximum.

Stress the scenario rather than guessing a maximum

Run the mortgage at the rate you have been quoted, then test a higher rate or a different term. If the household budget becomes uncomfortable under a modest change, that is important planning information even if the original payment looks manageable.

COSTS BEYOND THE MORTGAGE

Owning the property creates other commitments.

Mortgage affordability should leave room for insurance, maintenance, repairs, service charges where relevant, moving costs and taxes. Buying a property also involves transaction costs that do not disappear because the mortgage payment fits.

Use the calculator as a budgeting aid, not a substitute for a lender's formal affordability assessment or legal and property-specific advice.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Mortgage affordability FAQs

Should I use gross or take-home income?

For a household budget, take-home income is usually the more useful starting point because it reflects the money available after payroll deductions.

Does a larger deposit always mean I can afford a more expensive property?

It can reduce the amount borrowed, but you still need to keep enough cash for an emergency buffer and buying costs. A large deposit does not automatically make a high monthly budget comfortable.

Should I choose the longest mortgage term to lower the payment?

A longer term can reduce the monthly payment but may increase total interest and keep the debt outstanding for longer. Compare both payment and total cost.

Can the WorthChex result predict a lender's decision?

No. Lenders use their own affordability tests, underwriting, income evidence and other criteria.
