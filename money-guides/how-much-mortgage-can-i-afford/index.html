MORTGAGE GUIDE

How much mortgage can I afford?

Most UK lenders will lend around 4 to 4.5 times your yearly income. On a £40,000 salary that is about £180,000, which costs roughly £1,000 a month at 4.5% over 25 years. Whether that is comfortable depends on your take-home pay and other costs.

A lender's maximum is a ceiling, not a target. Many people aim to keep the mortgage payment below roughly 30–35% of take-home pay, so there is room for bills, food, childcare, saving and the occasional surprise.

Notice the pattern: borrowing the full 4.5× puts every salary above the 30–35% comfort zone, and the share rises with income because higher earners lose more of each extra pound to tax. That is why a lender can say “yes” to an amount that would leave a household stretched.

Joint example: two buyers earning £35,000 and £30,000 have a combined income of £65,000. At 4.5× they could borrow about £292,500, costing £1,626 a month. Their combined take-home pay is about £4,487 a month, so the mortgage would be 36% of it.

A bigger deposit also lowers your loan-to-value (LTV). Rates usually improve at each 5% step, most noticeably below 90%, 85%, 75% and 60% LTV, which can cut the monthly payment further.

WHAT LENDERS CHECK

It is not just the income multiple.

  • Your outgoings: lenders look at bank statements for childcare, loans, car finance, credit cards and regular spending. A £300 a month car payment can reduce the maximum loan by tens of thousands of pounds.
  • A stressed rate: lenders check you could still pay if rates rose above your starting rate.
  • Credit history: missed payments, high card balances and recent applications can reduce what you are offered.
  • Income type: bonuses, overtime and self-employed income are often only partly counted, or averaged over two years.
  • Higher multiples: some lenders go to 5 or 5.5 times income for higher earners or certain professions, usually with a bigger deposit.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Mortgage affordability FAQs

How much can I borrow on a £30,000 salary?

At 4.5 times income, about £135,000. At 4.5% over 25 years that costs £750 a month, against take-home pay of about £2,093.

Does a longer term help?

It lowers the payment but costs more overall. £180,000 at 4.5% costs £1,000 a month over 25 years (£120,150 interest) or £852 over 35 years (£177,782 interest).

Should I use gross or take-home pay?

Lenders use gross income for the multiple. For your own budget, take-home pay is what matters, because that is the money you actually have each month.

Can WorthChex tell me what a lender will offer?

No. Each lender has its own criteria. Our figures show what a loan would cost so you can decide what is comfortable before you speak to a lender or broker.
