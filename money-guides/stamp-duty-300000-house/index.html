How much Stamp Duty on a £300,000 house?

Stamp Duty Land Tax depends on the price and the buyer's circumstances. England and Northern Ireland use SDLT; Scotland and Wales have different property taxes.

STANDARD £300,000 EXAMPLE

The SDLT is calculated in bands, not at one rate.

Under the current standard residential bands, the first £125,000 is charged at 0%, the next £125,000 at 2%, and the final £50,000 of a £300,000 purchase at 5%. That produces £2,500 of SDLT under the standard scenario.

£0 to £125,000

0% under the standard residential bands.

£125,001 to £250,000

2% applies to this £125,000 portion.

£250,001 to £300,000

5% applies to the final £50,000 portion.

Calculation: £125,000 × 0% = £0; £125,000 × 2% = £2,500; £50,000 × 5% = £2,500. Standard SDLT therefore totals £2,500 on this simplified £300,000 scenario.

Official source: GOV.UK residential SDLT rates

BUYER STATUS CAN CHANGE THE BILL

£300,000 does not produce one SDLT result for everyone.

A qualifying first-time buyer may pay 0% on the first £300,000 under the current relief. An additional-property purchase can attract higher rates, while a non-UK-resident surcharge can also apply in qualifying circumstances.

Important: if you are a first-time buyer, do not simply assume that the standard £2,500 calculation is your answer. Check whether you qualify for the relief and whether the property price and joint-buyer circumstances meet the conditions.

JURISDICTION

Make sure you are using the right property tax.

Scotland uses Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales uses Land Transaction Tax. This guide is specifically about residential SDLT in England and Northern Ireland.

Specialist transactions such as linked purchases, corporate transactions, multiple dwellings or particular reliefs can require a different analysis.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

£300,000 Stamp Duty FAQs

How much SDLT is £300,000 for a standard buyer?

Under the standard residential bands, the simple calculation is £2,500.

How much would a qualifying first-time buyer pay?

Under the current relief and assuming the buyer qualifies, a £300,000 purchase falls entirely within the 0% first-time-buyer band.

Can an additional property cost more SDLT?

Yes. Higher rates can apply to additional dwellings. The exact outcome depends on the transaction and buyer circumstances.

Can the rules change?

Yes. Property tax rates and relief conditions can change, so use the official GOV.UK guidance for the current transaction.
