CAR FINANCE GUIDE

Should I pay cash or use finance for a car?

Neither option is automatically better. The useful comparison is between the total cost, the savings you keep, the monthly commitment you create and the other costs that come with owning the car.

START WITH THE TRADE-OFF

Cash reduces borrowing cost; finance can preserve cash.

Paying cash generally avoids the interest and borrowing charges that come with finance. But the money leaves your savings immediately. The important question is not only “How much does the car cost?” but “What will my financial position look like after buying it?”

Finance reverses that trade-off. You keep more cash available, but take on a recurring payment and potentially pay more than the cash price over the life of the agreement.

Cash purchase

Focus on the savings left after purchase. Check whether the remaining balance still covers the emergency buffer and any near-term expenses you already expect.

Finance purchase

Focus on the deposit, APR, term, monthly payment and total amount payable. A lower payment does not by itself mean a cheaper agreement.

Hybrid approach

A larger deposit can reduce borrowing while preserving some savings. Test several deposit amounts rather than assuming all available cash should go into the car.

COMPARE THE RIGHT NUMBERS

Four figures give you a clearer comparison.

When you look at a finance offer, compare the deposit, monthly payment, total amount paid and finance interest. Then compare the finance total with the cash purchase price and any fees.

Example: imagine a £20,000 car. A cash buyer may pay £20,000 and keep only the remaining savings. A finance buyer may put down £2,000 and keep more cash, but then pay a monthly amount for several years and a total above £20,000. Neither number alone describes the whole trade-off.

Do not forget the running costs

Insurance, fuel, servicing, tyres, parking, breakdown cover and repairs still exist whether the car is bought for cash or financed. Add those costs to your household budget when deciding whether the vehicle itself is affordable.

A PRACTICAL SEQUENCE

One way to compare your own situation

  1. Check the cash price and the finance illustration for the same vehicle.
  2. Work out how much savings would remain after a cash purchase.
  3. Compare the finance payment with monthly disposable income.
  4. Run a second scenario with a different deposit or term.
  5. Read the lender's actual contract for fees, final payments and early-repayment conditions.

Use the WorthChex Affordability Calculator for the household-budget question and the Car Finance Calculator for the borrowing-cost question.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Cash or finance FAQs

Does paying cash always save money?

It can avoid borrowing interest, but the comparison should include the opportunity cost of using savings and the value of keeping an emergency buffer available.

Is a lower monthly finance payment better?

Not automatically. Check the term and total amount paid. A longer agreement can reduce the payment while increasing the total finance cost.

Should I use all my savings as a deposit?

Not necessarily. Keeping an appropriate cash reserve may be important for unexpected costs. Test different deposit amounts against the emergency-fund level you want to maintain.

Does this guide replace a lender's finance illustration?

No. The calculator is a planning model. The lender's personalised illustration and contract should take priority for the actual agreement.
