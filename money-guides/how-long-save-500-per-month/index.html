SAVINGS GUIDE

How long does it take to save £500 a month?

Saving £500 each month adds £6,000 per year before interest. The time needed to reach a target depends on the starting balance, the consistency of the contributions and any growth assumption.

THE SIMPLE TIMELINE

With no starting savings and no interest, the maths is straightforward.

£5,000 target

10 monthly contributions of £500.

£10,000 target

20 monthly contributions of £500.

£20,000 target

40 monthly contributions of £500.

If you already have savings, subtract the starting balance from the target before dividing by £500. The Savings Calculator can model the exact result when the target does not divide evenly.

WHAT CHANGES THE ANSWER?

Starting money, interest and consistency all matter.

A positive interest assumption can increase the balance faster than contributions alone, but savings rates can change. If you want a conservative baseline, set growth to 0% and treat any positive-rate scenario as an illustration.

Example: if you already have £2,000 and want £10,000, only £8,000 remains. At £500 per month, the simple no-interest timeline becomes 16 months instead of 20.

Do not overlook withdrawals

The calculation assumes the £500 contribution continues and the savings already accumulated remain in the account. If you need to withdraw money for ordinary costs, the actual target date moves later.

IS £500 AFFORDABLE?

The timeline is only useful if the contribution fits your budget.

Check the contribution against housing, bills, food, transport, debt payments and irregular costs. The right monthly saving is not necessarily the largest amount you can manage once; it is the amount you can sustain while keeping enough breathing room.

Use the Affordability Calculator for the monthly-budget question and the Savings Calculator for the target timeline.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

£500-a-month savings FAQs

Is £500 a month enough to build an emergency fund?

It depends on your target and essential monthly costs. A contribution of £500 can build a buffer quickly, but the appropriate target is personal.

Does £500 a month become £6,000 exactly after a year?

Ignoring interest, twelve contributions of £500 equal £6,000. An account that pays interest would produce a different balance.

What if I miss one month?

At this contribution level, one missed £500 payment delays the target unless another deposit makes up the difference.

MAKE THE TIMELINE USEFUL

Turn the monthly contribution into a complete savings plan.

The headline £500 figure is only useful when it fits the rest of the household budget. Start with the contribution that can continue after rent or mortgage, utilities, food, transport, debt payments and irregular bills have been accounted for. Then use the target date to see whether the amount is sufficient.

£5,000 target

With no starting savings and no interest, ten monthly contributions of £500 reach £5,000. A starting balance or a missed contribution changes the date.

£10,000 target

With no starting savings and no interest, twenty monthly contributions reach £10,000. The Savings Calculator models the exact timeline when the target does not divide evenly.

£20,000 target

Forty monthly contributions of £500 reach £20,000 before any interest. At this size of goal, the opportunity cost of keeping the contribution sustainable becomes especially important.
