BUDGETING GUIDE

What to include in a monthly budget

A good budget lists every regular cost, including the ones that only come once a year. Below is a full checklist and a worked example for someone on a £30,000 salary, who takes home about £2,093 a month.

Last reviewed: October 2026Figures: worked out with the WorthChex calculators
THE CHECKLIST

Everything to put in your budget.

Home

Rent or mortgage, Council Tax, gas, electricity, water, buildings or contents insurance, service charge or ground rent.

Everyday

Food shopping, toiletries, phone, broadband, TV licence, streaming and app subscriptions, gym.

Getting around

Fuel or charging, car finance, insurance, tax, parking, train or bus fares.

Family

Childcare, school costs, pocket money, pet food and insurance.

Debts

Loans, credit cards, car finance, Buy Now Pay Later, overdraft interest.

Future you

Emergency fund, savings goals, pension top-ups, annual costs pot.

ANNUAL COSTS

Turn yearly bills into a monthly amount.

Irregular costs are the main reason budgets fail. Add them up and divide by 12:

Yearly cost (example)Per yearPer month
Car insurance£600£50
Car service and MOT£300£25
TV licence£180£15
Christmas and birthdays£480£40
Dentist and glasses£180£15
Holiday£900£75
Total£2,640£220

Paying that monthly figure into a separate “bills pot” means the money is there when the bill arrives.

A SIMPLE SPLIT

The 50/30/20 rule on £2,093.

Needs (50%)£1,047housing, bills, food, transport
Wants (30%)£628eating out, hobbies, holidays
Saving & debt (20%)£419savings and extra repayments

It is a starting point, not a rule. With high rent, needs may take 60–70%; that is common, and the answer is to keep wants lower rather than skip saving entirely.

FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Monthly budget FAQs

Should I budget from gross or take-home pay?

Take-home pay: the amount that actually lands in your account after tax, National Insurance, pension and student loan.

How do I budget with irregular income?

Base your budget on your lowest typical month, and put anything above that into a buffer account you draw from in quieter months.

How often should I review my budget?

Check it monthly for the first few months, then whenever something changes: a pay rise, a new bill, or a deal ending.
